China targets 40% electric heavy-truck sales by 2030

15 June 2026 • China
China set a national objective for electric vehicles to reach 40% of new heavy-truck sales and 20% of the heavy-truck fleet by 2030, or approximately 1.6 million vehicles. The policy direction is supported by charging and battery-swapping infrastructure, positioning commercial transport as a priority electrification segment. For manufacturers, battery suppliers and infrastructure operators, the target strengthens demand visibility across heavy-duty batteries, swap systems, grid upgrades and domestic truck production capacity.
Source: Reuters

Strategic Watch

China's heavy-truck electrification target signals a shift from passenger EV adoption toward commercial transport, where battery demand, charging infrastructure and fleet economics become the primary growth drivers. Companies supplying battery systems, battery swapping, charging infrastructure, power electronics and commercial vehicle platforms should monitor how provincial implementation plans, infrastructure investments and fleet procurement incentives evolve over the next 12–24 months. The pace of execution will determine whether the policy translates into sustained commercial orders or remains an aspirational target.

GreyRadius Insight

Heavy-duty transport has long been one of the most challenging segments to decarbonise because of high vehicle utilisation, payload requirements and long operating distances. If China succeeds in electrifying 40% of new heavy-truck sales by 2030, it will significantly accelerate demand for high-capacity battery packs, grid infrastructure and commercial charging networks while strengthening China's competitive position in global electric truck manufacturing and exports.

Uber, Nuro and Lucid to bring robotaxi service to Houston in 2027

17 June 2026 • United States
Uber, Nuro and Lucid announced plans to launch a robotaxi service in Houston in mid-2027, exclusively through the Uber network. The planned deployment extends the companies’ robotaxi programme beyond the San Francisco Bay Area and points to a broader platform strategy in which vehicle technology, autonomous systems, fleet operations and services revenue become part of the long-term mobility model.
Source: Uber, Nuro and Lucid

Strategic Watch

The Houston deployment should be viewed less as an autonomous vehicle pilot and more as a strategic ecosystem partnership combining vehicle manufacturing, autonomous driving technology and mobility platform operations. Investors and mobility companies should track fleet scale, regulatory approvals, safety performance and unit economics, as these factors will determine whether robotaxi services become commercially scalable rather than remaining technology demonstrations.

GreyRadius Insight

The partnership illustrates how the future mobility value chain is becoming increasingly collaborative. Vehicle manufacturers, autonomous technology developers and ride-hailing platforms are moving toward integrated business models where recurring mobility services may eventually generate higher long-term value than one-time vehicle sales. Success in Houston could influence similar deployments across North America and the GCC.

GM signals shift from LFP toward lithium-manganese-rich batteries

15–21 June 2026 • United States
General Motors indicated that future EV programmes may prioritise lithium-manganese-rich battery chemistry, while lithium iron phosphate batteries may be redirected toward stationary energy storage applications. Reuters reported that GM had previously planned to begin producing LFP cells at its Tennessee plant by late 2027, but GM’s battery leadership now expects LFP output to support energy storage while LMR becomes central to its EV battery roadmap. The position suggests a differentiated battery roadmap focused on cost, performance and supply-chain resilience. It also contrasts with the LFP strategies pursued by Tesla, Ford and Rivian for cost-sensitive electric vehicles.
Source: Reuters

Strategic Watch

GM's emphasis on lithium-manganese-rich (LMR) batteries reflects the growing importance of chemistry diversification as automakers balance cost, energy density and supply-chain resilience. Suppliers should monitor commercialization timelines, manufacturing readiness and raw material sourcing, as successful deployment could alter future demand for lithium, manganese, nickel and phosphate-based battery materials.

GreyRadius Insight

Rather than converging on a single battery chemistry, the EV industry is entering a phase of portfolio optimisation. Different chemistries will increasingly serve different vehicle segments and use cases. Manufacturers capable of managing multiple battery technologies are likely to gain greater flexibility in controlling costs, securing supply chains and responding to changing customer requirements.

Charging Infrastructure

BMW and Solarwatt announce EV-home energy management offer

15 June 2026 • Global
BMW Group and Solarwatt announced an integrated home energy management offer combining EV charging, stationary battery storage and household energy flows. The partnership illustrates how automakers are expanding beyond vehicle sales into distributed energy and home-energy optimisation. It also strengthens the role of EVs as part of a broader residential energy ecosystem.
Source: BMW Group

Strategic Watch

Automakers are increasingly extending their business models beyond vehicle sales into integrated energy ecosystems. Investors should monitor how vehicle-to-home technologies, residential battery storage, smart charging and energy management platforms influence customer retention, recurring service revenues and long-term ecosystem ownership.

GreyRadius Insight

Electric vehicles are evolving into distributed energy assets rather than standalone transportation products. As EVs become connected with household storage, rooftop solar and intelligent energy management systems, manufacturers that successfully integrate these capabilities can create differentiated customer experiences while unlocking new revenue opportunities across energy services.

Government Policy

UK Parliament examines EV transition effectiveness

15–21 June 2026 • UK
The UK Parliament Transport Committee continued its “Supercharging the EV transition” inquiry, examining EV uptake, incentives, eVED, second-hand EV markets and charging infrastructure. The review may influence future policy direction across EV mandates, consumer incentives and charging investment signals. For fleets and infrastructure investors, policy certainty remains a central condition for adoption and capital deployment.
Source: UK Parliament

Strategic Watch

The outcome of the parliamentary inquiry could shape future policy on consumer incentives, charging infrastructure investment, fleet electrification and the second-hand EV market. Businesses with exposure to the UK EV ecosystem should closely monitor policy recommendations, as regulatory clarity remains one of the strongest determinants of long-term investment decisions.

GreyRadius Insight

The UK has entered a phase where policy effectiveness is becoming more important than policy ambition. Future EV adoption will increasingly depend on predictable regulation, charging accessibility and consumer affordability rather than ambitious sales targets alone. Stable policy frameworks will be essential to maintaining investment confidence across manufacturers, fleets and infrastructure providers.
