Gulf data centre market entry strategy

The Gulf's data centre market is at a historic investment peak — with Saudi Arabia's $6B+ government cloud and data infrastructure commitment, UAE hosting 300+ data centres making it the Gulf's established cloud hub, and Qatar, Kuwait, and Bahrain all investing in national digital infrastructure. AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud have all established Gulf regions and government cloud procurement is accelerating across all GCC markets. International data centre operators, colocation providers, and hyperscaler wholesale companies are all evaluating Gulf market entry. GreyRadius helps data centre companies validate Gulf government and enterprise demand, navigate G-Cloud certification and data sovereignty requirements, and execute market entry.

Why now? Saudi Arabia's G-Cloud provider certification programme and UAE's Government Cloud strategy are both in active procurement phases — certified cloud and data centre providers gain access to government workload migration that will sustain for years. The data centre companies achieving G-Cloud certification in 2024-2026 will be positioned for the peak government cloud migration volumes in 2027-2030.

Market Intelligence

What the data says.

Gulf data centre market is projected to reach $10B annually by 2028 — government cloud migration, AI infrastructure demand, and enterprise digitalisation all driving 20% annual capacity procurement.

UAE is the Gulf's established colocation and cloud hub — AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud UAE regions creating enterprise demand for adjacent colocation and managed services that international operators can serve.

Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 AI and data programmes are creating GPU compute demand — SDAIA and NEOM AI infrastructure requirements are creating hyperscale compute demand that exceeds current Gulf capacity.

Data sovereignty is non-negotiable in Gulf government workloads — every GCC government has explicit data residency requirements that create structural demand for locally operated data centre infrastructure.

Market Reality

What makes this market hard.

  • G-Cloud provider certification is required for Saudi government cloud workloads — the certification process involves NCA security review, data residency validation, and service capability assessment taking 9-14 months.
  • Power infrastructure for large data centres requires advance coordination with Saudi and UAE utilities — ARAMCO, SEC, DEWA, and ADDC all have specific processes for large power offtake agreements.
  • ICV requirements apply to Saudi data centre contracts — local workforce, local spending, and local equipment procurement all factor into ICV scoring for government data centre contracts.
  • Competition from hyperscalers already present — AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud with government relationships and existing infrastructure — is creating a high competitive bar for new entrants.
