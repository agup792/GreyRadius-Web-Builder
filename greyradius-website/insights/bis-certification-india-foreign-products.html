BIS certification for foreign products entering India - what exporters need to know

Does BIS certification apply to your product? Mandatory certification lists, the foreign manufacturer pathway, Chinese-origin component questions, timelines and costs - explained for exporters entering India.

Market Entry India Regulatory August 2026 · 7 min read

What BIS certification is and when it is mandatory

The Bureau of Indian Standards operates mandatory certification schemes covering an expanding list of product categories - electronics and IT hardware, toys, steel products, appliances, footwear, chemicals and more. If your product appears in a mandatory list (under the ISI mark scheme or the Compulsory Registration Scheme for electronics), it cannot legally be imported or sold in India without certification. The lists expand through Quality Control Orders issued sector by sector, which means a product exempt last year may be covered this year - applicability must be checked against current orders, not past experience.

The foreign manufacturer pathway

Foreign manufacturers certify through the Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme: application, factory audit by BIS officials at your overseas plant, product testing in BIS-recognised labs, and an Indian authorised representative to hold local responsibility. Plan realistically for a multi-month process - audit scheduling for overseas plants is the common bottleneck - and treat the authorised representative decision seriously: that entity carries compliance obligations on your behalf.

The Chinese-origin component question

A recurring issue for Japanese, Korean and Southeast Asian exporters: products assembled at home with Chinese-origin components face additional scrutiny at Indian customs, and in some categories the component origin affects certification and import treatment. This is answerable - with component-level documentation, origin mapping and correct declarations prepared in advance. Discovered at port, the same question strands inventory; resolved in planning, it is paperwork.

Timelines, costs and the sequencing mistake

Certification timelines run from weeks (registration schemes with existing test reports) to several months (factory-audit routes for first-time applicants). The sequencing mistake we see repeatedly: companies sign distributors, book launch dates and then start certification - putting a regulator on the critical path of a commercial promise. The correct order runs certification applicability in the same phase as market validation, files early for covered products, and lets the launch calendar absorb reality instead of colliding with it.

Frequently asked questions

How long does BIS certification take for a foreign company?

From a few weeks under registration schemes (electronics with ready test reports) to several months under factory-audit routes - overseas audit scheduling is the usual bottleneck. File early and keep certification off your launch-critical path.

Do products with Chinese components need special treatment at Indian customs?

They face additional scrutiny in several categories, and origin can affect certification and duty treatment. Component-level origin documentation prepared in advance turns a port problem into paperwork.
