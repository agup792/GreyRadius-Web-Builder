Data Centres

Meta to invest C$13 billion in first Canadian AI data centre

8 July 2026 | Canada | Meta

Meta announced it will build its first Canadian data centre in Sturgeon County, Alberta, representing an investment of more than C$13 billion. The campus will launch with 1GW of IT capacity, making it one of the largest AI-focused data centre projects in the world, with potential expansion beyond that level. The facility is expected to create around 3,000 construction jobs, support more than 300 permanent roles, and includes approximately C$60 million for local infrastructure improvements. The investment reflects the continued race among hyperscalers to secure large-scale AI compute capacity.

Reuters

Strategic Watch

Track power procurement, grid interconnection, construction delivery and the potential expansion beyond the initial 1GW campus. These factors will determine how quickly the announced capacity becomes operational.

GreyRadius Insight

The Alberta project shows that AI infrastructure competition is increasingly shaped by access to large-scale power, land and local infrastructure. Hyperscaler capacity strategy is becoming inseparable from regional economic development.

Cloud Policy

UK brings major hyperscale cloud providers under direct financial-sector oversight

10 July 2026 | United Kingdom | AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft, Oracle

The UK Government designated Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, Microsoft and Oracle as the country’s first Critical Third Parties under the Financial Services and Markets Act. Effective 13 July 2026, the providers will be jointly supervised by the Bank of England, Prudential Regulation Authority and Financial Conduct Authority. The new framework introduces resilience testing, incident reporting and regulatory oversight for cloud services considered critical to the UK’s financial system, marking a significant step in cloud governance.

HM Treasury (GOV.UK) • Reuters

Strategic Watch

Financial institutions and cloud providers should monitor resilience testing expectations, incident reporting requirements and how joint supervision changes third-party risk management and procurement controls.

GreyRadius Insight

The designation moves cloud concentration risk from enterprise governance into direct regulatory supervision. Cloud resilience is becoming a board-level financial stability issue, not only a technology operating concern.

Infrastructure Investment

MasTec strengthens AI data centre construction business through US$1.65bn acquisition

7 July 2026 | United States / Global | MasTec, Superior Group

Infrastructure contractor MasTec announced the acquisition of electrical contractor Superior Group for US$1.65 billion. Superior employs approximately 3,000 people and is expected to contribute US$1.6–1.7 billion in annual revenue with adjusted EBITDA of US$225–250 million. The acquisition significantly expands MasTec’s capabilities in hyperscale data centre and AI infrastructure construction.

Reuters

Strategic Watch

Monitor integration execution, customer concentration, project backlog and whether the combined business captures growing demand for electrical and construction capacity across hyperscale AI projects.

GreyRadius Insight

AI infrastructure investment is creating value beyond chips and cloud platforms. Scarce construction, electrical and delivery capabilities are becoming strategic assets as data centre pipelines scale.

Sovereign AI

NVIDIA reinforces sovereign AI infrastructure strategy

Date not supplied | Global | NVIDIA

NVIDIA published new guidance highlighting how governments are investing in domestic AI infrastructure to support healthcare, scientific research, public services and national economic development. The company emphasized the growing importance of sovereign AI platforms that enable countries to retain control over strategic compute infrastructure while accelerating AI adoption across critical sectors.

Rafay

Strategic Watch

Governments and infrastructure partners should track national compute programs, procurement models, local data requirements and how sovereign platforms balance domestic control with dependence on global technology suppliers.

GreyRadius Insight

Sovereign AI is shifting from policy language to infrastructure strategy. Control over compute, data and deployment capacity is becoming part of national competitiveness and public-sector resilience.
